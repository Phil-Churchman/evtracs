Captured vehicle tracking

How recorded GPS journeys are cleaned, matched onto the road network and summarised — the data a calibration scenario is checked against.

What goes in
Recorded fixes
device_locations.xlsx
Road network
roads.graphml

The fixes come off the tracker app as timestamped latitude and longitude, with an accuracy column if the export carries one. The network is the same graph the simulation routes over, so a matched journey and a simulated one are measured against the same roads.

The cleaning pipeline 9 stages

  1. Load and screen the points

    Read the captured fixes in time order. Where the export has an accuracy column it is used; where it does not, the later stages do the filtering on their own.

  2. Find where the vehicle dwells

    Detect places the vehicle repeatedly sits still — a rank, a home base, a lunch stop. Points within 20 m of each other group together, a group counts as a dwell once it holds 20 points inside 5 minutes, and centroids within 100 m merge into one location.

  3. Flag excursion loops

    Mark the points that leave a dwell and come back within 20 minutes. That is manoeuvring around a stop rather than travelling, and left in it would read as a journey.

  4. Collapse the flagged runs

    Replace each continuous flagged sequence with a single start-and-end pair, so an hour parked becomes two points rather than several hundred.

  5. Match the points onto roads

    Build a spatial index over the network's edges and a drivable subgraph, then walk the points in order, snapping each to a road within 20 m. Candidates are scored on distance plus half their heading difference: footways and cycleways take a +100 penalty so the track stays on roads, and staying on the edge already being followed earns −10, which stops the match flickering between parallel ways.

  6. Patch the route between fixes

    Route along the network between consecutive snapped points to fill the gap, up to 50 edges and 5 minutes apart. Past that the link is recorded as invalid rather than a route being invented.

  7. Validate the speed

    Work out the speed implied by each matched hop and flag it when it exceeds twice the speed limit for that highway type — 50 km/h on residential, 130 on motorway, and so on. Flagged hops keep their row but contribute no distance.

  8. Run it three times

    The matching repeats, each pass re-matching only the points that matched on the last one. The route settles rather than being dragged by fixes that were never going to match.

  9. Summarise each road used

    Count how many times every edge was traversed and work out its average and maximum observed speed, writing all three back onto the network that is exported.

What comes out

gps_noise_reduced.xlsx

The cleaned points, each carrying its snapped position and speed, the edge it matched to, the list of edges traversed to reach it, and the two flags the later steps set — whether the route link was valid and whether the speed was unrealistic.

matched_routes.graphml

Just the part of the network the vehicle actually used, each edge carrying its traversal_count, average_speed_kmh and max_speed_kmh. This is what makes the observed speeds comparable with the model's assumed ones.

Seeing the result Animation Track viewer

The tracked route animation takes those two files and replays the journey along the matched road geometry rather than jumping between raw fixes. Time is distributed along each patched route by distance, so the vehicle moves at the speed the data implies.

Any stop longer than a minute is held for about half a second and then skipped, with a banner while it holds — otherwise a day's playback is mostly waiting. Hovering a road shows its average speed and how many times it was driven.

It plays from 1× to 1200×, and steps point by point or an hour at a time. The GPS track viewer beside it shows the same matched tracks against the network without the playback.

The cleaning script and both viewers live in Model/tracker_data_processing/.

Where it is used

This is the input side of a calibration scenario. The observed per-road speeds are compared against the simulated ones and the assumed speeds adjusted until the two agree; the result becomes the global road speed table every scenario then runs with.

The same tracked journeys also give the distance and fare-wait distributions a distribution scenario draws from. The calibration workflow puts both in context.
